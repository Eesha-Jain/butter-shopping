# Grocery & shopping app market research

Deep competitive research for Butter’s Shop experience. Focus: what shoppers love, hate, and expect from grocery/shopping apps — and what an ideal UX looks like when you synthesize the patterns.

**Sources (2023–2026):** App Store & Google Play reviews; Reddit (`r/instacart`, `r/Safeway`, `r/walmart`, `r/Target`, `r/Costco`, `r/HEB`, `r/traderjoes`, `r/Frugal`, `r/venmo`, `r/UXDesign`, `r/amazonfresh`, `r/kroger`, `r/Portland`); Baymard Institute grocery UX research; consumer reviews (The Kitchn, NBC Select, Money Crashers, Clark.com); store/corporate product pages.

Ratings below are approximate snapshots and move over time.

| # | App | Role | Rough signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Instacart | Multi-store delivery marketplace | Convenient; fee/tip/substitution rage on Reddit |
| 2 | Amazon Fresh | Habit-first grocery + Prime | Buy Again praised; Amazon cart complexity hated |
| 3 | Target | Retail + Drive Up + Circle | ~4.9 App Store; Drive Up ritual loved |
| 4 | Walmart | Grocery + pickup/delivery + maps | Strong filters/value; produce & stock trust issues |
| 5 | Safeway | Loyalty + coupons + local grocery | Savings loved; coupon UX roasted |
| 6 | Kroger family (Fred Meyer / QFC) | Coupons, lists, aisle, pickup | Useful when working; list/coupon bugs common |
| 7 | H-E-B (My H-E-B) | In-store companion gold standard | Aisle sort + store map frequently praised |
| 8 | Costco | Membership, warehouse, bulk | Digital card/receipts useful; inventory untrusted |

## 1. Instacart

**What it is:** Multi-retailer grocery marketplace. Shoppers (gig workers) pick and deliver from stores you choose. Instacart+ membership (~$9.99/mo or ~$99/yr) reduces delivery fees.

### Pros (20)

1. Order from many stores (and categories beyond groceries) in one app.
2. Delivery to your door when you cannot leave home (illness, kids, no car, disability).
3. Real-time chat with the shopper for substitutions and produce notes.
4. Item-level substitution preferences before checkout.
5. Broad national coverage vs single-retailer apps.
6. Access to stores you might not shop in person (including Costco via Instacart in many markets).
7. Same-day and scheduled delivery windows.
8. Family / shared account options on membership plans.
9. “Buy again” / past-order shortcuts for repeat staples.
10. Add-to-cart from list/search without always opening a full PDP (Baymard-aligned pattern).
11. Order tracking from shop → checkout → delivery.
12. Refund/credit flow for missing or damaged items is relatively familiar to users.
13. Promo codes and membership trial offers lower the first-order barrier.
14. Some retailers show true in-store pricing (users specifically seek these).
15. Can send groceries to another address (help family, gifts).
16. Large catalog that usually mirrors store shelves more closely than specialty apps.
17. Useful for large basket trips that would be exhausting in-store.
18. Shopper photos of receipts/items in some flows increase transparency.
19. Multi-store mental model teaches users they can “shop around” digitally.
20. Strong muscle memory: once users adopt it, replenishment becomes a habit.

### Cons (20)

1. Stacked fees (service, delivery, bag, “regulatory,” etc.) can add ~10–15%+ before tip.
2. Tip defaults feel aggressive (often ~15–20%); tip pressure is a constant Reddit theme.
3. Many stores mark up items vs in-store shelf price.
4. Membership is almost required for frequent use to feel “worth it.”
5. Missed retailer sales, clipped coupons, and loyalty discounts vs shopping in-store.
6. Produce quality is out of your hands; soft fruit / bad greens are a top complaint.
7. Substitutions can be absurd or brand-wrong (internet folklore + real reviews).
8. You must stay glued to your phone if you want approval on replacements.
9. Delivery windows slip; late orders after paying for a specific slot.
10. Shopper skill varies wildly; you cannot reliably pick your shopper.
11. Out-of-stock claims when the item is on the shelf (shopper couldn’t find it).
12. Sponsored / promoted products crowd search results.
13. Weaker loyalty integration than retailer-owned apps.
14. Hard to overview a large mobile cart before checkout.
15. Tip + fees can erase any “sale” savings you thought you had.
16. Return/credit friction has increased for some users (photo proof, credits not cash).
17. Heavy tip culture creates awkwardness for budget shoppers (students especially).
18. Opaque fee naming (“service,” “regulatory response”) erodes trust.
19. Not a good price-comparison engine across stores for the same SKU.
20. Feels like paying a convenience tax every time — resentment builds even when the product “works.”

## 2. Amazon Fresh

**What it is:** Amazon’s grocery layer (Fresh / Whole Foods on Amazon), optimized for Prime members who reorder staples. Strong “Buy Again / Past Purchases” patterns studied by Baymard.

### Pros (20)

1. Buy Again / Past Purchases make replenishment extremely fast for returning users.
2. Habit carousels often appear early — grocery is transactional, not exploratory (Baymard).
3. Add from past purchases without hunting order history buried in Account.
4. Price comparison vs last purchase helps spot markdowns on familiar items.
5. Prime bundling: grocery rides alongside packages users already trust.
6. Delivery scheduling and reserved slots in dense cities.
7. Subscribe / Repeat Items for weekly staples without full Subscribe & Save friction.
8. Huge catalog when Fresh + Amazon general merchandise mix.
9. One account/wallet for grocery + everything else.
10. Markdown hunting via past orders is a real power-user tactic on Reddit.
11. Product cards with image + price + quick add match grocery mental models.
12. Quantity can often be adjusted without opening a heavy PDP.
13. Familiar Amazon return/refund vocabulary for non-perishables.
14. Whole Foods pickup options in some markets.
15. Strong search for branded packaged goods (when Fresh catalog is healthy).
16. Saved addresses and payment methods reduce checkout typing.
17. Useful for apartment dwellers who already get Amazon deliveries.
18. “Add all from past order” accelerates full weekly restocks.
19. Trust brand for logistics (even when grocery ops differ from packages).
20. Sets the industry expectation that staples live on the home screen.

### Cons (20)

1. Fresh cart vs general Amazon cart confusion — items land in the wrong fulfillment path.
2. Alexa / Lists → Fresh cart handoff is incomplete or confusing for new users.
3. “Shop this item” can surface many lookalikes instead of the exact SKU last bought.
4. Past Purchases sometimes polluted with items users never bought (recent Reddit reports).
5. Feature churn: Past Purchases / Buy Again placement changes and breaks habits.
6. Reservations / slots can be scarce in high-demand cities.
7. Produce quality still depends on a picker you don’t control.
8. Search and save-for-later weaker than pure grocery apps in comparative UX notes.
9. Prime required or strongly assumed — awkward for non-Prime students.
10. Sponsored rows and Amazon retail chrome compete with grocery tasks.
11. Harder to see true local grocery competition (Safeway sale vs Fresh price).
12. Substitutions and partial orders create last-mile anxiety.
13. UI complexity: too many Amazon surfaces (Fresh, Whole Foods, Subscribe & Save).
14. Dietary filters less trustworthy / less prominent than specialty grocery apps.
15. Large orders are hard to audit before placing.
16. Delivery instructions ignored more often than users expect.
17. Price opacity when Fresh price ≠ nearby supermarket sale.
18. Ad-blocker / web quirks can break Past Purchases for desktop users.
19. Feels optimized for Amazon’s catalog logic, not aisle logic.
20. When Buy Again is buried under promos, users bounce to Walmart/Target (Baymard quote pattern).

## 3. Target

**What it is:** Retailer app with grocery + household + style. Signature fulfillment: Drive Up (free curbside to your car). Loyalty: Target Circle; paid: Circle 360 (includes Shipt-style same-day delivery). App Store rating often ~4.9.

### Pros (20)

1. Drive Up is free with no order minimum — rare among competitors.
2. In-app prices generally match in-store (high trust vs Instacart markups).
3. Fast ritual: place order → “I’m on my way” → team brings bags to the car.
4. High-contrast CTAs and relatively clear primary actions vs coupon mazes.
5. Target Circle offers and earnings apply to Drive Up for many users.
6. Circle Card 5% discount stacks with habitual shopping.
7. Aisle / location hints help in-store findability.
8. Barcode scan for price and product info.
9. One cart for groceries + household + personal care (true one-stop).
10. Sticky cart / fulfillment state machine is simpler than multi-fee marketplaces.
11. Same-day delivery option via Circle 360 / Shipt when you need door drop.
12. Student pricing exists on Circle 360 in some offers (~half monthly).
13. Stock check before you waste a trip (even if imperfect).
14. Price match policies (within windows) reduce buyer’s remorse.
15. Returns via Drive Up reduce friction.
16. Starbucks add-on with Drive Up in some stores (delightful edge case).
17. Apple Pay / wallet checkout is fast.
18. App Store reviews repeatedly call navigation “quick and easy.”
19. Drive Up reduces in-store impulse spend for disciplined shoppers.
20. Strong brand trust: people *want* to open Target more than most grocery apps.

### Cons (20)

1. Inventory “available” then INF’d (item not found) at pick — top guest complaint.
2. Search cluttered by promotions / Circle pushes over relevance.
3. App sometimes auto-assigns fulfillment mode (pickup vs Drive Up vs delivery) confusingly.
4. Search bar not persistent on every screen (reviewers backtrack).
5. Pickup readiness windows can be vague (“within X hours”) vs exact time.
6. Packaging for perishables not always insulated for a long drive home.
7. Grocery depth weaker than a full supermarket for some categories.
8. Circle offer UI can feel like another coupon homework layer.
9. Same-day delivery still involves tip / shopper variability via Shipt rails.
10. Frequent redesigns of Drive Up tooling frustrate both guests and TMs (Reddit).
11. Squishmallow / hype-item stock fights create rage reviews that pollute grocery signal.
12. Substitutions and partial picks still happen on delivery orders.
13. Promo ranking in search “more than is reasonable” (App Store review theme).
14. Cart overview still gets crowded with suggestions.
15. Not great for multi-store price optimization (Target-only catalog).
16. Circle 360 cost (~$99/yr) may not pay off for light shoppers.
17. Occasional glitches checking in for Drive Up.
18. In-store map/aisle detail is not as precise as H-E-B’s store map praise.
19. Dietary filtering less central than specialized grocery tools.
20. When inventory fails, free Drive Up still costs time and trust.

## 4. Walmart

**What it is:** Mass retailer grocery + general merchandise. Pickup, delivery, shipping mix. Walmart+ for unlimited delivery perks. Strong category filters; store maps; Scan & Go.

### Pros (20)

1. Everyday low prices — strong for budget / student shopping.
2. Free or low-friction curbside pickup (no tip required).
3. Walmart+ unlimited delivery over thresholds for frequent orderers.
4. Reorder / “my items” accelerates weekly staples.
5. Filter chips (including dietary) are comparatively strong in grocery UX notes.
6. Two-column grids with add-on-card support fast building of carts.
7. Store maps help find items in cavernous stores.
8. Barcode scanner for price checks and list building.
9. Walmart Pay / Scan & Go speeds in-store checkout.
10. Easy to price-compare from home before committing.
11. Can mix pickup groceries with ship-from-warehouse for OOS staples.
12. Refunds for missing items are often easier than Amazon returns for food.
13. Gas discounts with Walmart+ add tangible membership value.
14. Real-time order tracking on homepage.
15. Pharmacy + grocery in one ecosystem.
16. Large national footprint — almost always a nearby store.
17. “Save for later” helps resist impulse adds before submitting pickup.
18. Sparky AI assistant (newer) for Q&A / shopping help.
19. Clear department browsing for users who hate search.
20. Pickup correctness is often better than delivery in Reddit anecdotes.

### Cons (20)

1. Delivery produce quality frequently called out as rotten / crushed.
2. Inventory says in stock, then cancelled or substituted at pick.
3. Substitutions default to more expensive or wrong size items.
4. Substitution notifications sometimes email-only — easy to miss.
5. Delivery instructions ignored by drivers.
6. Heavy items stacked on bread/eggs → damage.
7. Search can return overly broad / irrelevant results.
8. “My items” randomly drops staples or replaces with wrong variants.
9. Pickup vs ship confusion when an item isn’t store-available.
10. App bloat: VR home, ads, Paramount, gas — grocery path competes with chrome.
11. Customer service for tiny missing items can become call-center bureaucracy.
12. Variable picker quality by store/time of day.
13. Sponsored products in search.
14. Walmart+ still may not feel worth it for tiny baskets.
15. Store maps can be wrong after remodels.
16. Dietary needs require vigilance — labels inconsistent across private brands.
17. Large carts hard to audit on mobile.
18. Delivery tip culture still present even with membership.
19. Latency / crashes reported in busy periods.
20. Emotional brand baggage: some users hate the *store* even when the app is fine.

## 5. Safeway

**What it is:** Albertsons-family supermarket app for deals, Just for U personalization, pickup/delivery, and in-store coupon clipping. Seattle-area relevance is high for Butter.

### Pros (20)

1. Real savings when digital coupons and weekly ads apply correctly.
2. Just for U personalized deals can beat generic flyer pricing.
3. Local store selection matches what you’ll actually find nearby.
4. Pickup + delivery without needing a third-party marketplace.
5. Ability to check weekly ad / sale items before planning meals.
6. Scanner for deals while walking aisles (when it works).
7. Loyalty account ties gas / points in some markets.
8. Past purchases / reorder for regulars.
9. In-store price is the “real” price (no Instacart markup layer).
10. Useful for clipping a stack of deals before a big trip.
11. Recipe / meal inspiration occasionally tied to sale items.
12. Store locator with hours and services.
13. Digital receipt history for returns and budgeting.
14. Familiar supermarket categories (meat, produce, dairy) done thoroughly.
15. Campus-adjacent Safeway locations make it a default student store in many cities.
16. When search works, branded grocery SKUs are findable.
17. Coupons can make the app feel “worth downloading” vs generic lists.
18. Delivery for mobility-limited shoppers without learning Instacart.
19. Fuel rewards motivation for car owners.
20. Strong “I saved $X” feedback loop when clipping succeeds.

### Cons (20)

1. Coupon clipping feels like homework — endless Load More / multi-tap.
2. Weekly-ad “scan to clip all” features removed; friction increased (App Store + Reddit).
3. “No Deals Found” loops while standing in aisle with bad cell/Wi‑Fi.
4. Tiny fonts — users literally beg “MAKE FONTS BIG AGAIN.”
5. App crashes, freezes, lag, logout loops.
6. Cart quantity glitches; carts wiping.
7. Coupons fail at register → cashier conflict and line shame.
8. Barcode scan opens manufacturer web pages instead of the deal.
9. Must scan shelf tag, not item barcode, for some coupons — unintuitive.
10. Coupon expiry dates printed unreadably small.
11. Inventory for pickup frequently wrong → substitutions.
12. Delivery search misses sale/coupon items users can see in-store.
13. BOGO and complex promos often don’t apply as expected online.
14. Notes for shoppers / replacement rules too limited.
15. Home screen is information soup; deals scattered across entry points.
16. Forced app engagement for the “real” price angers customers and staff.
17. Tip + delivery fee stack after you already “saved” with coupons.
18. Accessibility issues for older shoppers (core Safeway demographic).
19. Constant redesigns break muscle memory.
20. Trust erosion: when coupons lie, the whole brand feels adversarial.

## 6. Kroger family (Fred Meyer / QFC)

**What it is:** Shared Kroger digital platform under local banners. Digital coupons, weekly ads, lists with aisle hints, fuel points, pickup/delivery. Critical for Pacific Northwest (QFC, Fred Meyer).

### Pros (20)

1. Digital coupons + weekly ads in one place for the store you actually use.
2. Fuel points are a tangible, habit-forming reward.
3. Shopping lists with aisle location (when data is good).
4. Pickup orders often apply coupons more reliably than chaotic in-store clipping.
5. Past purchases / reorder for staples.
6. Pharmacy integration in many banners.
7. Kroger Pay / mobile checkout options.
8. Cross-banner coupon overlap for many national brand offers.
9. Personalized deals and cash-back style offers in newer iterations.
10. Store-specific pricing and ad zones (when location is correct).
11. Ability to plan meals around Wednesday coupon drops (power-user tip).
12. Receipt history and return support via app.
13. Large catalog including general merchandise at Fred Meyer.
14. Search that *can* show aisle on results (praised when it works).
15. Good for “I’m already in this ecosystem” households.
16. Delivery/pickup without learning a separate marketplace UX.
17. Alt ID / phone number checkout path for people without the physical card.
18. Boosts / personalized savings can stack for engaged users.
19. National engineering investment (not a tiny indie app).
20. When stable, it’s a complete supermarket companion: list → aisle → coupon → pay.

### Cons (20)

1. Lists fail to add items, show “Product Info Not Available,” or desync across devices.
2. Checking off one list item checks off others (QFC review theme).
3. Aisle sort broken or generic after remodels / bad data.
4. Coupons disappear or won’t apply in-store despite “clipped.”
5. Login / store-select infinite loops blocking pickup.
6. Crashes and freezes (especially iPad reports).
7. Search returns nothing until rephrased magically.
8. Cart icon / delivery icon confusion — checkout affordances vanish (Fred Meyer reviews).
9. Constant redesigns destroy muscle memory (“designed by committee”).
10. Microscopic coupon tiles and mis-tap-prone carousels.
11. Everything jammed on page one so nothing is one tap.
12. Delivery UI buries the real cart behind odd icons.
13. Ingredient info missing on many allergy-critical products (older complaint, still relevant).
14. Tip + delivery fee on top of “savings.”
15. Employees themselves warn customers the app is unreliable (review anecdotes).
16. Location wrong → wrong ad/prices.
17. Slow performance / lag on mid phones.
18. Notifications more marketing than operational (substitution alerts weak).
19. Feature parity uneven across QFC vs Fred Meyer vs Kroger naming.
20. Reliability debt: users keep the app only because the store is local, not because UX is good.

## 7. H-E-B (My H-E-B)

**What it is:** Texas-regional supermarket app widely cited (including by TJ shoppers elsewhere) as the in-store companion gold standard: lists sorted by store geography, store map pins, coupons, curbside.

### Pros (20)

1. Shopping list sorted by aisle / store geography — reduces backtracking.
2. Store map with product location pin (not just “Aisle 7”).
3. Build list all week at home, then execute a fast in-store run.
4. Easy coupon / DigiDeal discovery tied to real products.
5. Price and unit-price comparison while browsing.
6. Sort/filter by price; “buy it again” filter inside search.
7. Weekly ad browsing without walking the whole store.
8. Curbside / delivery options when you don’t want to enter.
9. Pharmacy management (refills, prepay) useful for families/students abroad.
10. Product search that answers “do you even carry this?”
11. Strong local brand love → higher forgiveness and engagement.
12. Recipe → add ingredients flows (when present) save planning time.
13. Subtotal visibility on lists helps budget before checkout.
14. Scan coupons instead of managing paper stacks.
15. Works across multiple H-E-B locations you rotate between.
16. Partner/staff culture of pointing customers to the app for findability.
17. Clearer information hierarchy than many national chain apps (user sentiment).
18. Reduces forgotten items because the list is the route.
19. Good balance of in-store companion + online fulfillment (aspirational model).
20. Proves grocery apps can feel helpful instead of extractive.

### Cons (20)

1. Regional only — irrelevant outside H-E-B footprint (not a national template you can install everywhere).
2. Aisle data wrong during remodels → route lies.
3. Users want bay numbers (Home Depot-style); aisle alone still hunting.
4. Auto store detection imperfect; wrong store = wrong map.
5. Filters often allow only one active filter at a time.
6. Online/curbside pricing may include service fees (e.g. % fee called out in reviews).
7. List sharing with family historically requested / limited.
8. Clear-all list / cart management can feel buried.
9. Coupon stacking rules confusing (app won’t auto double-stack).
10. Delivery/curbside quality varies like any picker model.
11. App fatigue: another store-specific download.
12. Search can still overwhelm with variants for simple staples.
13. Map feature needs precise indoor data maintenance — expensive to keep honest.
14. Occasional buggy coupon/cart states (App Store “improved but not 100%” theme).
15. Less useful if you split trips across HEB + another chain (no multi-store optimizer).
16. Produce still needs human judgment in-store — app can’t replace eyes.
17. Notifications can skew promotional.
18. Account/login friction for light users who just want a list.
19. Feature discovery: power tools (buy-it-again filter) are easy to miss.
20. Sets a high bar: once users try HEB-quality aisle routing, every other grocery app feels broken.

## 8. Costco

**What it is:** Membership warehouse club app — digital membership card, warehouse inventory hints, online shop, gas prices, pharmacy, lists. Shopping model is bulk + treasure-hunt, not weekly produce staples for everyone.

### Pros (20)

1. Digital membership card when you forgot your wallet — repeatedly cited as “worth installing.”
2. Years of digital receipts for returns and expense tracking.
3. Warehouse inventory checker *exists* (better than nothing).
4. Gas price comparison across nearby warehouses.
5. Pharmacy refill convenience.
6. Shopping lists to resist $200 impulse carts.
7. Online ordering for items not in your warehouse.
8. Executive rebate / membership value visibility.
9. One-step scan membership + pay flows in newer versions.
10. Strong unit economics for households that can store bulk.
11. Reliable brand trust on returns culture (warehouse).
12. Useful trip planning: “which warehouse has X?”
13. Price monitoring on big-ticket / seasonal items.
14. Reduces paper clutter (card + receipts).
15. Clear separation in users’ minds: app for logistics, warehouse for treasure hunt.
16. Helps students/roommates plan rare bulk runs.
17. Order history for online purchases.
18. Location-aware warehouse finder.
19. Less coupon-homework theater than Safeway/Kroger.
20. When it works, it’s a utilitarian toolkit — not a social feed.

### Cons (20)

1. Inventory “in stock” / “low stock” widely distrusted; people drive 40 minutes for ghosts.
2. Inventory lag (backroom, -1 quirks, overnight sync) vs floor reality.
3. Search returns irrelevant results or fails to find carried items.
4. Crashes on launch — catastrophic when you need the digital card at the door.
5. Passkey / login nag loops.
6. App feels like a wrapped mobile website to many users.
7. Warehouse vs online price confusion.
8. No precise aisle map comparable to H-E-B.
9. Membership paywall before any shopping value.
10. Bulk model mismatched to dorms / small kitchens (product, not just UX).
11. List features thinner than dedicated list apps.
12. Delivery/shipping experience separate from warehouse magic.
13. Treasure-hunt business model conflicts with perfect digital inventory transparency.
14. Slow performance.
15. Wrong default warehouse / delivery location.
16. Accessibility / font / density complaints on product grids.
17. Customer service for app bugs feels distant vs warehouse staff helpfulness.
18. Limited dietary filtering for specialty needs.
19. Push toward online when users wanted warehouse confirmation.
20. Expectation gap: users assume Costco’s ops excellence extends to the app — it often doesn’t.

## 9. Flipp

**What it is:** Weekly ad / circular aggregator across grocery and retail. Search an item → see which nearby flyers have it on sale. Lists, coupon loading to loyalty cards, favorites. Not a full checkout grocery store.

### Pros (20)

1. All local weekly ads in one place — kills mailbox clutter.
2. Search across flyers (“chicken thighs”) to find the cheapest sale nearby.
3. Clip deals to a shopping list in one tap.
4. Collaborative / shareable grocery lists.
5. Favorite stores filter reduces noise.
6. Loyalty cards wallet — fewer plastic cards.
7. Digital coupon loading to store loyalty programs.
8. Coupon + flyer matchup hints for power savers.
9. Covers grocery + pharmacy + big-box circulars.
10. Quantity fields on list items.
11. Location-based relevance (when geolocation is correct).
12. Free to use (ads-supported).
13. Deal watchlists for items you buy rarely.
14. Helps multi-store trip planning for frugal households.
15. Visual circular browsing still available for people who like flyer UX.
16. Faster than opening 5 retailer apps just to compare ads.
17. Good “Sunday planning” ritual tool.
18. High App Store rating historically (~4.8) for the category.
19. Teaches price awareness — users learn what “good” costs.
20. Complements retailer apps instead of replacing store choice.

### Cons (20)

1. Not a store catalog — can’t check real-time stock or aisle.
2. No checkout / pickup / delivery — you still need another app or a trip.
3. No cash-back rewards vs Ibotta-like apps.
4. Active work required; not passive savings.
5. Shows stores an hour away unless you discipline filters.
6. Flyer zone mismatches: ad shown ≠ that store’s zone promo (Reddit PSA).
7. Sale previews sometimes too small to read store/price.
8. Missing flyers when retailers pull ads.
9. Encourages multi-store hopping that costs gas/time — false economy.
10. List UX can feel clunky vs AnyList-style tools.
11. Coupon availability uneven by retailer.
12. Ad-heavy feeling for users who only shop 2–3 stores.
13. Online-only deal coverage weaker than browser coupon tools.
14. Doesn’t remember your personal “good price” history well (user wish).
15. Overwhelming for students who just need one campus store.
16. Stale deals if circular dates / regions mis-tagged.
17. Privacy/location sensitivity for deal targeting.
18. Switching cost: planning in Flipp, buying in Safeway/Walmart — context lost.
19. Can train obsessive deal-chasing over dietary/quality goals.
20. Alone, it cannot be “the” shopping app — only a savings layer.

## 10. Venmo

**What it is:** Peer-to-peer payments (PayPal). Not a grocery app — included because Butter’s shared hauls / party split map to how students already settle grocery money. UX lessons transfer to in-app split.

### Pros (20)

1. Near-zero learning curve for young adults — already installed.
2. Pay or request in seconds without exchanging bank numbers.
3. Split requests to multiple people from one bill.
4. Transaction history doubles as a lightweight receipt log.
5. Emoji/notes make mundane splits socially legible (“🥦 Costco”).
6. Instant transfer options when someone needs cash now.
7. Debit/bank funding without writing checks.
8. Huge network effects in US college friend groups.
9. Business profiles / QR for small sellers (edge cases).
10. Privacy controls exist (public / friends / private) if configured.
11. Low or no fee for standard bank-funded friend payments.
12. Push notifications create gentle payment reminders.
13. Searchable payment history for “did I already pay you?”
14. Works for rent, utilities, *and* grocery — one mental model.
15. Group trip / party expense patterns are familiar.
16. Faster than cash for awkward small amounts ($3.75 oat milk).
17. Desktop/web less necessary — mobile-native habit.
18. Identity is phone/username — easy to find roommates.
19. Recurring social norm: “just Venmo me” removes negotiation friction.
20. Proves money UX can feel light — grocery split shouldn’t feel like TurboTax.

### Cons (20)

1. Social feed / public-by-default history feels like financial exhibitionism.
2. You don’t always control privacy of payments *others* send you.
3. Scams and fake “wrong payment, send it back” patterns are common.
4. Friend payments generally irreversible — weak buyer protection.
5. Credit-card funding fees punish the wrong funding source.
6. US-only — bad for international students’ cross-border friends.
7. Group split still clumsy when amounts differ per person.
8. Built-in calculator has been removed/broken in updates (Reddit anger).
9. Can’t paste calculator amounts into the $ field reliably (classic UX bug).
10. Pay vs Request composition flow has long-standing keyboard/focus bugs.
11. Primary action (pay/request) can feel visually secondary to the social feed.
12. Balance and bank settings buried in menus.
13. No structured “grocery trip object” — just freeform notes.
14. No itemization tied to a shared cart/list (Butter opportunity).
15. Tip culture / payment awkwardness still exists socially.
16. Chargebacks and frozen funds horror stories erode trust.
17. Merchant checkout via Venmo lacks credit-card style protections.
18. Notification spam and debit-card upsell modals.
19. Incomplete for true household budgeting (no envelopes, categories).
20. Using Venmo *outside* the shopping app creates context switching and lost item-level fairness.

## Cross-app patterns (synthesis)

### What users consistently love

- **Habit-first shopping:** Buy Again / past purchases above the fold (Amazon Fresh, Baymard).
- **One-tap add → quantity stepper** on the card (not forced PDP).
- **Honest local price** (Target, Walmart pickup, retailer apps) vs markup marketplaces.
- **Fulfillment clarity:** Drive Up / curbside as a simple state machine.
- **In-store spatial help:** aisle sort + map (H-E-B).
- **Savings without homework:** auto-applied deals > clip scavenger hunts.
- **Trusted list check-off** that never lies.
- **Split money that feels as easy as Venmo**, but tied to the actual haul.

### What users consistently hate

- Fee stacks + tip pressure after “convenience.”
- Fake inventory and chaotic substitutions.
- Coupon mazes, tiny type, committee home screens.
- Sponsored search crowding out the item you typed.
- Unreliable lists (add/check-off/sync bugs).
- Privacy-invasive or upsell-heavy chrome.
- Apps that gate the real price behind engagement theater.

## What an ideal shopping app looks like (intensive UX)

This section is the product north star: a composite of Baymard grocery research, App Store/Play anger, Reddit workarounds, H-E-B spatial strengths, Target fulfillment clarity, Amazon habit mechanics, Flipp price awareness, and Venmo’s social settlement speed — aimed at Butter’s campus shopper.

### 1) Job to be done (in order)

1. **Replenish staples** in under two minutes.
2. **Plan a trip** (or order) that respects budget, diet, and schedule.
3. **Execute** in-store or via pickup/delivery without anxiety.
4. **Settle money** if the haul was shared.
5. **Learn** only after the basics are safe (discovery is a separate mode).

Anything that inverts this order (promo theater before staples) is anti-ideal.

### 2) Home: one calm composition, habit first

- **Above the fold:** search + store/context + **Staples / Buy again** carousel with product image, price, and `+`.
- After add, `+` becomes a **quantity stepper** (minus to zero, editable qty for double digits, live cart total).
- **Sticky cart summary** (count + $) always visible.
- At most **one** secondary carousel (e.g. “On sale from your staples”) — not five promo banners.
- Discovery (“trending on campus”) lives in its own tab/mode so it never blocks replenishment.
- New users without history see **categories + popular campus staples**, not an empty void of ads.

### 3) Search & browse: respect the query

- Persistent search on shop surfaces.
- Results ranked by **relevance to the store you selected**, with **Buy again** and **In stock here** boosted.
- Sponsored rows **clearly labeled** and capped; never 5–10 ads before organic hits.
- Filter chips that matter for food: diet (vegan, halal, GF…), price, sale, brand, package size — **multi-select**, session Clear all.
- Add from the grid; PDP is optional for nutrition/allergens, not mandatory for milk.

### 4) Product truth: labels, allergens, recalls

- Diet and allergen flags are first-class, not buried.
- Ingredient change / recall alerts on *your* staples.
- Unit price (per oz / per each) beside shelf price.
- Sale badges are short and honest (“$1 off through Tue”) — not a clip wall.

### 5) List: boringly correct (the trust core)

- Add, qty, check-off, undo — **never** check the wrong row, never ghost-add.
- Offline-tolerant check-off for bad store Wi‑Fi.
- Shared household/party list with presence (“Alex added eggs”).
- Modes:
  - **Plan** (home): categories or recipes.
  - **Store** (companion): **sort by aisle / path**, map pin when data exists.
- Estimated subtotal always visible; budget ceiling warning before you overrun.

### 6) Multi-store intelligence (Butter differentiator)

- Compare **basket total** across nearby stores (campus market, Safeway, Target…) including travel time, not just line-item min.
- Recommend **shop today vs wait for sale day** from real prices + coupons — without making coupon clipping a mini-game.
- Auto-apply public discounts; optional eligibility programs opt-in, never dark-patterned.

### 7) Fulfillment: a state machine, not a maze

- User-chosen mode: **In-store list**, **Pickup**, **Delivery**, **Split trip**.
- Clear promises: ready-by time, fee line-items **itemized before tip**, substitution policy chosen up front.
- Substitution UI: prefer **skip** / **specific alt** / **chat** — never surprise mushrooms-for-tampons energy.
- Inventory honesty: prefer “likely low” over fake green checkmarks; learn from user “wasn’t there” reports.

### 8) In-store companion mode

- Large type, huge hit targets, one-handed use while holding a basket.
- Scanner that finds **price + deal + aisle**, not a manufacturer webpage.
- Path list that updates as you check off; haptics on successful add/check.
- No logout loops, no microscopic coupon tiles at chest height in a cold aisle.

### 9) Checkout & money

- Cart overview scannable: image, name, qty stepper, price, sale state.
- Fees transparent; tip optional and explained (who receives it).
- Confirmation before any automatic/habit order fires.
- **Shared haul split:** item-aware split (even / by item / custom) with one tap settle — Venmo-simple, grocery-smart; private by default (no social feed of oat milk).

### 10) Notification ethics

- Operational > promotional: substitution approval, pickup ready, price drop on staples, recall.
- Quiet hours; batch marketing.
- Never require email to catch a substitution.

### 11) Accessibility & performance

- Dynamic type; high contrast; VoiceOver labels on steppers.
- Fast cold start; lists survive flaky campus Wi‑Fi.
- No hostage popups (“download to see price”) — the app *is* the experience.

### 12) Emotional design (campus-specific)

- Respect tight budgets and dining-plan adjacency.
- Don’t shame deal-skipping; don’t force gamified clipping.
- Make the first weekly shop feel like **control**, not a second job.
- Delight is subtle: haptic add, clear savings summary, a path that gets you out of QFC in 15 minutes — not confetti for watching a video to unlock $0.20.

### Ideal app scorecard (use this in critiques)

| Dimension | Ideal bar |
| --- | --- |
| Time to reorder staples | < 2 minutes |
| Adds requiring PDP | Optional for most SKUs |
| List reliability | Zero wrong check-offs |
| Price trust | App price = payable price |
| Fee clarity | All fees visible before place order |
| Substitution control | Chosen before shopper/picker acts |
| In-store findability | Aisle/path sort + large type |
| Diet safety | Filters + alerts on staples |
| Shared pay | Item-aware, private, one-tap |
| Cognitive load | ≤ ~4 primary choices on Shop home |

## Implications for Butter Shop

1. **Staples first** — habits/purchases above the fold (not a favorites table buried under promos).
2. **One-tap add** from grid; `+` → qty stepper; no PDP required for the fast path.
3. **Trustworthy filters** — auto-apply food rules; easy override; session-only Clear all.
4. **Honest sale badges** — short promo labels on cards, not clip scavenger hunts.
5. **Reliable list** — boringly correct add/qty/check-off per active group list; shared party lists.
6. **Trending ≠ staples** — campus discovery separate from personal replenishment.
7. **Calm UI** — Hick’s law: ~3–4 primary choices; ≤2 carousels; 3–4 category chips + More.
8. **Spatial consistency** — product = bottom sheet; filters = right panel; haptics on successful add.
9. **Multi-store totals** — compete with Flipp’s awareness + retailer honesty, without Instacart fee despair.
10. **Venmo-grade split, grocery-grade fairness** — settle shared hauls inside the trip object, private by default.
11. **Fulfillment honesty** — prefer pickup/list clarity (Target-like) over opaque marketplace fees.
12. **Companion mode later** — H-E-B-level aisle sort is a moat once store data allows; until then, don’t fake inventory.
